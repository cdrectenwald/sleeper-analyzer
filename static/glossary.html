Fantasy Football Glossary

Understanding your stats like a pro

Luck Score

The difference between your actual wins and your expected wins based on all-play record. A positive luck score means you won more games than your points suggested you should have. A negative score means the fantasy gods were cruel to you.

Example: You went 10-4 but your all-play record was 8-6. Your luck score is +2. You got lucky matchups where your opponents had bad weeks when they played you.

All-Play Record

How you would have done if you played every team every week instead of just your scheduled opponent. This is the truest measure of how good your team actually was.

Example: In a 10-team league, you "play" 9 games per week (against everyone else). If you scored the 3rd most points in Week 1, you'd go 7-2 in all-play for that week.

Expected Wins

Based on your all-play record, this is how many games you "should" have won. It's calculated by taking your all-play win percentage and multiplying by actual games played.

Example: 60% all-play win rate over 14 weeks = 8.4 expected wins.

Points For (PF)

Total points your team scored across all weeks. The most basic measure of team quality.

Bench Points

Points scored by players on your bench who didn't start. High bench points might indicate good depth or poor start/sit decisions.

Weekly Scoring

Individual player points for a specific week. We track starters vs bench and can show which players carried your team (or let you down).

Manager vs Player Stats

Manager stats = Team-level performance (wins, luck, total points).
Player stats = Individual NFL player performance within your league's scoring.
